GoodDock

Sit. Stay. Good boy.

Your Dock wanders between monitors. GoodDock teaches it to stay on the one display you choose.

Download GitHub

Free · macOS 13 or later

Sound familiar?

With more than one display, the Dock never stays put. Push the pointer to the bottom of any screen and the Dock follows it there. You lower the mouse for a second, and the Dock has moved to the next monitor. To bring it back, you push again.

macOS offers two settings, and neither solves it. Turn off "Displays have separate Spaces" and the Dock stays put, but every monitor now switches Spaces together, which is worse. Leave it on and Spaces stay independent while the Dock wanders.

GoodDock fills the gap. Spaces keep working per display, and the Dock stays where you put it.

Features

Pick your display visually

Click a scaled map of your actual monitor layout instead of guessing from names.

Follows your setup

Detects monitors being connected, disconnected, or rearranged.

Stays out of the way

One small menu bar icon. It takes no space in the Dock itself.

Reversible anytime

Choose "Don't pin" or quit the app, and the default behavior returns instantly.

Launch at login

Turn it on once, and it is waiting after every restart.

No network access

Nothing leaves your Mac. The only stored value is the display you picked.

How it works

macOS has no API to pin the Dock to a display. So instead of moving the Dock, GoodDock removes the condition that makes it move.

The Dock is summoned when a pointer already resting at the bottom edge of a screen is pushed down once more. GoodDock watches for that push on displays you did not pin, and nudges the pointer a few pixels back up. The pointer can never sit against the bottom edge, so the Dock is never called.

Edges where another display continues below are excluded automatically, so moving the pointer between monitors is never affected.

Install

  1. Download the DMG from Releases and open it.
  2. Drag GoodDock into your Applications folder.
  3. The first time, right-click the app and choose "Open", then confirm with "Open" once more.
  4. Allow Accessibility permission. (System Settings > Privacy & Security > Accessibility)
  5. Click the dog in the menu bar and pick the display where the Dock should stay.

Step 3 is needed because this app is not signed with an Apple Developer certificate. If it still refuses to open, clear the quarantine attribute:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/GoodDock.app

Note: "Displays have separate Spaces" (System Settings > Desktop & Dock > Mission Control) must stay on. Turning it off ties every monitor to the same Space, which is the very problem this app exists to avoid.

Questions

Why does it need Accessibility permission?
To notice the moment your pointer reaches the bottom edge of a screen, the app has to watch system mouse events, and macOS governs that through Accessibility. GoodDock reads cursor position only. It never reads keystrokes.

Will it slow my Mac down?
Handling one mouse event takes about 11 nanoseconds. Even moving the mouse frantically, that works out to roughly 0.001% of one CPU core.

How do I uninstall it?
Quit the app and move it to the Trash. It never changes your system settings, so the Dock goes back to its usual behavior immediately.
